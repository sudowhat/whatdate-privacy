Privacy Policy — WhatDate

Last updated: 20 September 2026

Applies to: WhatDate for Android, version 0.47.0 and later

The short version

WhatDate keeps your reminders on your phone.

  • There is no account. No sign-up, no login, no email, no phone number, no user ID.
  • There is no WhatDate server. We do not run one. There is nowhere for your data to be sent to us, so we never receive it, and we could not look at it even if we wanted to.
  • We collect nothing. No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking of any kind.
  • Nothing leaves your phone on its own. Every single case where something leaves is something you personally started — sharing with a friend, saving a backup, or tapping a link preview. Each one is listed below.

Is there an account?

No.

You do not create an account to use WhatDate. There is no password to WhatDate itself, nothing to sign in to, and nothing to sign out of. We have no user list, because there are no users to list.

When you add a friend in WhatDate, your two phones exchange a key directly with each other. That is what a “friend” is here — an agreement between two phones, not a record on a server. Nobody, including us, holds a directory of who is friends with whom.

What is stored, and where

Everything you put into WhatDate is stored on your phone only:

  • your reminders and events — titles, notes, dates, times, repeat rules, tags
  • anything you attach to them — photos, links, locations
  • your reminder settings, sounds, and preferences
  • your profile — the name and photo you set
  • your friends list

This is kept in a database on your phone that is encrypted, with the key held in your phone’s own secure hardware storage. If you turn on App Lock, it also needs your fingerprint or face to open.

We have also switched off Android’s automatic cloud backup for WhatDate. Your phone will not quietly copy your WhatDate data to Google Drive. The only backup is one you make yourself (see below).

What leaves your phone, and when

This is the complete list. Nothing happens in the background, and nothing happens without you doing it.

1. Sharing a reminder with a friend who is near you

What leaves: the reminder you chose to share.

Where it goes: straight to your friend’s phone over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

When: only while you have “Also deliver when we’re near each other” switched on, and only to people you have already added as friends.

This is phone-to-phone. It does not use the internet and does not pass through any company’s servers — not ours, not anyone’s.

2. Sharing a reminder as a message

What leaves: the reminder you chose to share, as a file or a link.

Where it goes: into whichever app you pick to send it — WhatsApp, email, Signal, whatever you choose.

When: only when you tap Share and choose an app.

Once you hand it to another app, that app’s own privacy policy applies to it, not ours. We have no control over, and no visibility into, what happens after that.

3. Your profile (your name and photo)

What leaves: the display name and photo you set in Settings → Profile.

Where it goes: only to people you have added as friends, so they see who a reminder came from. It travels the same two ways as above — phone-to-phone when near, or in an invite you send.

When: when you send someone an invite, and when your profile reaches friends you already have.

Your name is included in an invite so the other person can see who is asking. Your photo is never put in the QR code.

Nobody who is not your friend ever receives your profile.

4. Link previews

What leaves: the web address (URL) you saved, sent to that website.

Where it goes: directly to the website itself — for example, if you save a link to a news article, WhatDate asks that news site for the page title and picture.

When: only if you are using the online version of the app and you have turned “Offline only” off. It ships on, so link previews are off until you choose otherwise.

When this happens, that website can see your phone’s IP address, the same as if you had opened the link in your browser. WhatDate sends no cookies, no account, and no identifier with the request — only the address and a label saying the request came from WhatDate. Nothing about the fetch comes back to us.

5. Voice input

What leaves: what you say, while you are using the microphone button.

Where it goes: to the speech recognition service built into your phone. On most Android phones that is Google’s, and it may process your speech on Google’s servers, under Google’s privacy policy, not ours.

When: only while you are actively using the voice button. The microphone is not open at any other time.

WhatDate never receives, records, or stores your audio. It only gets back the text and turns it into a reminder. This part of Android is outside our control, which is why we are telling you about it plainly — if you would rather it never happen, don’t use the voice button.

6. Backups you make yourself

What leaves: a backup file containing your data.

Where it goes: wherever you choose to save it — your phone’s storage, an SD card, or a cloud drive if you pick one.

When: only when you tap Backup and choose a location.

The file is encrypted with a password you choose, using strong encryption (AES-256-GCM). We cannot recover that password, and neither can anyone else. If you lose it, that backup cannot be opened, by you or by us.

If you choose to save the backup to a cloud drive, that cloud provider’s privacy policy then applies to the file — though the contents stay encrypted with your password.

That’s everything. No other data leaves your phone at any time, for any reason.

What never leaves your phone

Some things are local-only by design and are never included in anything shared, even when you share the reminder they belong to:

  • Reminders marked Private. These are never put into any share, any invite, or any transfer to a friend.
  • Photos, files, and locations you attach to a reminder. Attachments stay on your device and are never sent to friends.
  • Your notes about who shared what with you, and your reminder history.

The two versions of WhatDate

There are two builds of this app:

  • The offline version has no internet permission at all. Android itself will not let it open a network connection, whatever the app tries to do. Link previews simply do not exist in it.
  • The online version has internet permission, used for one single thing: fetching link previews (section 4 above). It also ships with “Offline only” switched on, which blocks even that until you turn it off yourself.

Adding friends and sharing with them does not need the internet in either version. It works phone-to-phone.

Children

WhatDate is not aimed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect information from anyone — children included — because it does not collect information at all.

Your rights, and deleting your data

Because your data only exists on your phone, you are already in full control of it:

  • To see it: open the app.
  • To delete one item: delete it in the app. Deleted items sit in Recently Deleted for 30 days so you can undo a mistake, then they are permanently removed.
  • To delete everything: uninstall WhatDate. That removes the app’s data from your phone. Any backup files you saved yourself will still exist wherever you put them — delete those separately if you want them gone.

We cannot delete your data for you, because we do not have it. There is nothing for us to delete, and no request you need to send us.

Third-party components

WhatDate includes standard software libraries for things like QR scanning, nearby connections, and database encryption. These run entirely on your phone. None of them collect data about you or send anything to their makers.

WhatDate contains no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no tracking libraries of any kind.

This page itself loads no fonts, scripts, or images from anywhere else, so reading it does not tell any third party that you were here.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the “Last updated” date at the top will change with it. If we ever change what leaves your phone, we will say so plainly here rather than burying it.
